## Purpose
NEWS2 aggregates seven physiological parameters (respiration rate, SpO2, supplemental oxygen, systolic blood pressure, pulse, consciousness, temperature) into a score from 0 to 20. The score drives how quickly a clinician must review the patient.

## Response thresholds
- Aggregate 0-4: routine monitoring; the RPM nurse reviews trends at the next scheduled check.
- Any single parameter scoring 3 (red score): urgent review by the RPM nurse within 30 minutes to decide whether a physician is needed.
- Aggregate 5-6: urgent response. A physician-level review within 60 minutes is expected, with an increased monitoring frequency.
- Aggregate 7 or more: emergency response. Immediate physician review and consideration of emergency transfer.

## Partial scores from wearables
A wrist wearable usually measures only pulse and SpO2. A partial score can under-estimate risk, so it should never be read as reassurance. When a partial score is rising, request the missing readings (blood pressure, temperature, respiratory rate) from the patient or from home devices before concluding that the patient is stable.

## SpO2 Scale 2
Patients with confirmed hypercapnic respiratory failure (commonly COPD) have a prescribed target saturation of 88-92 %. Use SpO2 Scale 2 for them. Saturations above target while on oxygen score points, while 88-92 % on air scores zero.

## Trend over time
A rise of 3 or more points within an hour is clinically meaningful even when the absolute score is still low. It should prompt an earlier review and more frequent measurement.
