## Highest-risk period
The first 7-14 days after discharge carry the highest risk of deterioration and readmission. Risk factors include:
- a heart failure, COPD, sepsis or stroke admission
- two or more admissions in the previous year
- a long length of stay
- discharge to a place other than home without support
- polypharmacy

## Routine actions
1. Make a structured follow-up call within 72 hours: symptoms, medication reconciliation, and understanding of warning signs.
2. Arrange a clinician follow-up within 7 days for high-risk patients.
3. Tailor monitoring thresholds to the discharge diagnosis and baseline observations.

## Using risk scores
Model-based risk scores support prioritisation. They are not decisions, and a score from an unvalidated model must be treated as weak evidence. Clinical signs and patient-reported symptoms always take precedence.
