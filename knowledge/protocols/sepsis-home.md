title: Suspected sepsis in a patient at home (demo protocol)
---
## Red flags
Treat sepsis as possible when an infection is suspected together with one or more of:
- new confusion or reduced alertness
- respiratory rate of 22/min or more
- systolic blood pressure of 100 mmHg or less
- heart rate above 130/min
- SpO2 below 92 % on air
- no urine output in 18 hours
- mottled or ashen skin

## Amber flags
Temperature of 38 °C or above, or below 36 °C, heart rate of 91-130/min, a recent procedure, or immunosuppression. On their own, amber flags mean a same-day clinical review.

## Actions for the RPM team
1. Treat red flags as time-critical and escalate to the on-call physician immediately. Consider emergency transfer, because antibiotics and fluids should start within one hour of recognition.
2. Ask the patient to confirm symptoms (fever, rigors, breathlessness, confusion) and obtain a full set of vital signs.
3. Record the time sepsis was first suspected; this timestamp is audited.

## Recently discharged patients
Patients discharged after a sepsis admission have a high risk of recurrence and readmission within 30 days. Their thresholds for review should be lower.
