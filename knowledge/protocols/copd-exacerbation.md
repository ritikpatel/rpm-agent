---
## Target saturation
Many COPD patients have a prescribed target of 88-92 %. Readings in that range are expected and should not trigger low-SpO2 alarms. Use personalised thresholds and NEWS2 SpO2 Scale 2.

## Signs of exacerbation
Worsening breathlessness, more or discoloured sputum, new wheeze, a respiratory rate above the patient's usual level, and a heart rate rising with dyspnoea.

## Actions
1. If SpO2 falls below 88 % on repeated spot checks, or the respiratory rate rises by more than 6/min from baseline, the RPM nurse calls the patient and reviews their self-management plan (rescue medication as prescribed).
2. If SpO2 falls below 85 %, or there is drowsiness, confusion or inability to speak in sentences, escalate to the on-call physician immediately. Hypercapnia may be developing and emergency assessment is needed.
3. Confirm the reading is genuine (warm hands, still arm, good signal quality) before acting on a single value, but never delay escalation when symptoms are present.
